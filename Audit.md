BEHAVIORAL AUDIT – SOUNDFLOW PREMIUM

1. Project Overview

Application: SoundFlow Premium

Version: A – Original Interface

Objective: Identify potential behavioral design issues in the original subscription page and define evidence-based improvements to be implemented in Version B.

The application allows users to explore and compare four music subscription plans. The original interface includes subscription cards, pricing information, promotional buttons, a feature comparison section, and FAQs.

This audit evaluates how the interface may influence users' attention, comprehension, and subscription decisions.

2. Behavioral Issues Identified

Issue 1 – Choice Overload and Cognitive Load

Observation (Version A):

Users encounter four subscription alternatives, multiple features, promotional elements, and detailed information on a long page.

Behavioral theory:

Choice overload and cognitive load. When users face numerous alternatives and attributes, comparing options can become mentally demanding.

Proposed improvement (Version B):

Introduce a simple plan-comparison guide.

Display three key attributes for each subscription: price, number of users, and main benefits.

Organize additional information in expandable sections.

Preserve access to all four plans.

Hypothesis:

Simplifying the comparison process will reduce decision effort and help users identify the most suitable subscription.

Issue 2 – Salience and Visual Attention

Observation (Version A):

Bright green call-to-action buttons and promotional labels attract attention, potentially making certain elements more visually prominent than the information required to compare plans.

Behavioral theory:

Salience bias and attentional effects. Visually distinctive elements can disproportionately influence which information users notice.

Proposed improvement (Version B):

Establish a clearer visual hierarchy.

Give pricing, eligibility, and essential benefits sufficient visibility.

Ensure that promotional labels do not obscure important information.

Use consistent visual treatment across subscription options.

Hypothesis:

A more balanced presentation will encourage users to compare plans based on relevant attributes rather than visual prominence alone.

Issue 3 – Framing of Subscription Costs

Observation (Version A):

Subscription offers include prominent promotional messages and pricing information. The visibility and comprehension of billing conditions require verification.

Behavioral theory:

Framing effect. The way subscription costs and benefits are presented can influence users' perceived value.

Proposed improvement (Version B):

Clearly display the monthly subscription cost.

Make promotional duration and subsequent charges explicit whenever applicable.

Present renewal and cancellation conditions close to the subscription button.

Avoid wording that could create misleading impressions about total costs.

Hypothesis:

Transparent pricing information will improve users' understanding of subscription conditions and reduce incorrect expectations.

Issue 4 – Decision Support and Personalization

Observation (Version A):

Users must assess which of the four plans best fits their situation by comparing the available information.

Behavioral theory:

Decision support and cognitive ease. Structured guidance can reduce the effort required to evaluate alternatives.

Proposed improvement (Version B):

Add a short, optional plan-selection guide.

Ask users about the number of intended listeners and relevant eligibility conditions.

Recommend a suitable plan based on their answers.

Allow users to review and select any plan independently.

Hypothesis:

Simple, transparent decision support will increase the likelihood that users identify a plan consistent with their actual needs.

3. Expected Behavioral Outcomes

Version B is expected to:

Reduce the effort required to compare subscription alternatives.

Improve users' understanding of pricing and renewal conditions.

Reduce the influence of potentially distracting visual elements.

Increase users' confidence in their final subscription choice.

These are predictions, not observed results.

4. Evaluation Plan

To evaluate whether Version B performs better than Version A, both interfaces should be tested using equivalent decision tasks.

Suggested task: Ask participants to identify the most suitable subscription for a specified user profile and explain their choice.

Evaluation metrics:

Decision time: how long users take to select a plan.

Decision accuracy: whether the selected plan matches the stated requirements.

Price comprehension: whether users correctly understand subscription charges.

Perceived confidence: how confident users feel about their decision.

The objective is to improve decision quality, not simply to increase subscription conversions.

5. Pre-Implementation Commitment

The changes described above constitute the planned behavioral redesign from Version A to Version B.

This audit must be committed to GitHub before implementation of Version B begins.

All subsequent modifications and AI-generated prompts will be documented in PROMPTS.md.
