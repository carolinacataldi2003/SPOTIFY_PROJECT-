RECOMMENDATION – SOUNDFLOW PREMIUM

1. Executive Summary

The objective of this project was to improve the decision architecture of SoundFlow Premium's subscription page by applying behavioral science principles.

Version A presented users with four subscription alternatives, promotional information, and detailed plan descriptions. Although this provided multiple options, the original design potentially increased cognitive effort and made certain information more salient than others.

Version B was developed around four behavioral interventions identified in the pre-implementation audit:

Reducing choice overload.

Improving information salience.

Introducing more transparent pricing frames.

Providing personalized decision support.

Our primary recommendation is to prioritize informed decision-making over simply maximizing subscription conversions.

2. Key Recommendations

Recommendation 1 – Simplify Subscription Comparisons

Behavioral principle: Choice overload and cognitive load.

The original interface required users to process multiple subscription attributes simultaneously.

Version B introduces a more structured comparison, emphasizing essential information such as pricing, number of users, and key benefits while keeping additional details accessible.

Expected impact:

Reduce cognitive effort.

Facilitate comparisons between subscription plans.

Help users identify the most appropriate alternative more efficiently.

Recommendation 2 – Improve Information Salience

Behavioral principle: Salience and attentional effects.

Prominent promotional messages and call-to-action buttons can disproportionately attract users' attention.

Version B aims to establish a clearer visual hierarchy, making essential information more noticeable without excessively highlighting particular subscription options.

Expected impact:

Improve attention to decision-relevant information.

Reduce the influence of potentially distracting elements.

Encourage more balanced comparisons.

Recommendation 3 – Increase Pricing Transparency

Behavioral principle: Framing effect.

How subscription prices and promotional conditions are presented can influence users' perceptions of value.

Version B prioritizes clearer communication of monthly prices, promotional conditions, and renewal terms, where this information is available.

Expected impact:

Improve understanding of subscription costs.

Reduce misunderstandings regarding promotional offers.

Support more informed and transparent decisions.

Recommendation 4 – Introduce Personalized Decision Support

Behavioral principle: Cognitive ease and decision support.

Users may struggle to determine which subscription best matches their personal circumstances.

Version B incorporates an optional plan-selection guide designed to help users evaluate available options based on their needs and eligibility.

Expected impact:

Reduce the effort required to identify a suitable plan.

Improve alignment between user needs and subscription selection.

Increase perceived confidence in the decision.

Importantly, users should retain full freedom to select any available plan, regardless of the recommendation provided.

3. Evaluation and Expected Outcomes

The effectiveness of Version B should be evaluated through a controlled comparison with Version A.

Participants should be randomly assigned to one of the two versions and asked to complete equivalent subscription-selection tasks.

These outcomes represent hypotheses, not experimentally verified results.

4. Limitations and Trade-offs

Several limitations should be acknowledged:

No empirical validation: The predicted behavioral improvements have not yet been established through controlled user testing.

Potential oversimplification: Reducing visible information may cause users to overlook secondary features relevant to their preferences.

Recommendation bias: Personalized suggestions may unintentionally influence users toward a recommended option even when alternative plans would be suitable.

Context dependency: The effectiveness of behavioral interventions may vary across user segments and purchasing situations.

Therefore, Version B should not automatically be considered superior simply because it incorporates behavioral design principles.

5. Final Recommendation

We recommend adopting Version B as the preferred candidate for user testing, rather than immediately replacing Version A.

The redesigned interface is expected to improve subscription decisions by reducing cognitive complexity, increasing information transparency, and helping users identify plans aligned with their needs.

However, implementation should be followed by empirical testing to determine whether these improvements translate into measurable behavioral outcomes.

Version B should be preferred only if it demonstrates meaningful improvements in decision quality and user comprehension without compromising transparency or freedom of choice.

The ultimate goal of the redesign is not to influence users toward a particular subscription, but to make it easier for them to choose the subscription that best fits their needs.
